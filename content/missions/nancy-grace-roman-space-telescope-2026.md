# Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope

## Why it happened

Roman is designed to study cosmic expansion and planets beyond our solar system through wide-field infrared surveys. Its Wide Field Instrument combines a large view of the sky with detailed imaging. A separate Coronagraph Instrument tests techniques for blocking a star’s light to reveal nearby planets. The observatory is named for NASA’s first chief astronomer, Nancy Grace Roman. <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/">NASA mission overview</a>.

## The mission

Roman launched on 30 August 2026 aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center’s Launch Complex 39A. Its destination is the Sun–Earth L2 region, with a transfer lasting about three months. NASA leads the mission, with contributions from ESA, JAXA, France’s CNES, and Germany’s Max Planck Institute for Astronomy. <a href="https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasas-dark-universe-seeking-nancy-grace-roman-space-telescope-launches/">NASA launch report</a>.

As of 29 October 2026, Roman is undergoing commissioning. NASA reported on 22 October that it had entered science commissioning after checking the observatory and its primary instrument. Test observations are being used to calibrate the instruments and refine image quality before routine science operations. <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/roman/2026/10/22/nasas-roman-space-telescope-enters-final-commissioning-phase/">NASA commissioning update</a>.

The planned science mission lasts five years. Its telescope has a 2.4-metre primary mirror. <a href="https://science.nasa.gov/mission/roman-space-telescope/observatory-technical/">NASA technical specifications</a>.

## Results

The launch was successful and commissioning is in progress. The planned surveys have not yet begun; their scientific goals should not be treated as completed discoveries.
