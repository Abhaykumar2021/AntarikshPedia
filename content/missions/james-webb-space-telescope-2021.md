# James Webb Space Telescope

## Why it happened

By the mid-1990s, Hubble's deep fields had shown that long exposures on an
empty patch of sky filled with galaxies and raised the question of what
an instrument tuned to infrared would see, since the light of the earliest
galaxies has been stretched out of the visible band by cosmic expansion.
NASA's 1996 decadal planning embraced a next-generation space telescope with
a mirror far larger than Hubble's 2.4 m, which meant one thing: it had to
fold to fit inside a rocket fairing.

## The mission

JWST launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 from Kourou, French Guiana.
Over the following two weeks it executed the most complex deployment ever
attempted by a spacecraft: sunshield pallets lowered, five kapton membrane
layers tensioned apart, the deployable tower extended, then eighteen
hexagonal beryllium mirror segments unfolded and aligned to nanometer
precision. All 344 single-point-of-failure deployments worked.

The telescope settled into a halo orbit around Sun–Earth L2, 1.5 million km
from Earth, where its sunshield keeps the mirrors below 50 Kelvin. First
science images were released on 12 July 2022.

## Results

The SMACS 0723 deep field, Webb's first public image, recorded thousands of
galaxies in a patch of sky subtending a grain of sand held at arm's length,
with some objects' light emitted over 13 billion years ago. Subsequent
observations have characterized exoplanet atmospheres molecule by molecule,
resolved star formation inside dust clouds opaque to visible light, and
measured galaxy masses early enough in cosmic history to constrain how fast
the first generations of stars formed.
